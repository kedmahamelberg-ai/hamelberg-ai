Research note · 23 November 2026

When 147 articles describe 140 developments

In late October the Observatory collected six more AI articles than the week before. Counting the developments behind those articles changes the increase to one. Counting only new developments reverses the direction.

What changed between weeks 43 and 44?

Article volume rose from 141 to 147 (+4.3%). Developments represented in the coverage rose from 139 to 140 (+0.7%). Developments marked new fell from 136 to 133 (−2.2%). An editor tracking workload could reasonably report a busier week. An editor tracking new developments would have a different headline from the same collection.

How to reproduce the comparison

Open a release and find its counts object. Read ai_relevant_articles, ai_relevant_event_records and new_event_records. For the last column subtract represented developments from AI articles, then divide by AI articles. In week 44 the seven additional articles account for 7 ÷ 147, or 4.8%. They can add reporting or corroboration. This measure does not classify them as redundant.

Does a smaller sample give the same answer?

Weeks 44 and 46 both have seven articles above the one-per-development baseline. The share rises from 4.8% to 5.5% because week 46 has fewer articles. Reporting only the larger percentage would hide the unchanged count. Conversely, reporting only the count would hide how much of each week’s reading it represents.

Check your own collection

Count each included article once and assign it to exactly one development. Use the same inclusion rules and dates for both fields.

Where this comparison stops

A returning development can appear in more than one week, so adding the four weekly event counts would overstate the number of distinct developments across the full period. The resolver also leaves ambiguous cases separate. If two records later merge, the article count can stay fixed while the calculated coverage share rises. These figures describe the selected release revisions and the outlets the Observatory captured. They cannot estimate the total number of AI developments worldwide.

Source and method: the archived releases linked in the table and the Observatory methodology. This desk analysis used AI assistance to compare published counts. Read the guide to counting missing evidence for cases where some records could not be classified.
