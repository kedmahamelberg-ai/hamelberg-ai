About this work

One publisher. Three questions about evidence and society.

Hamelberg AI brings together independent public research and reporting created and developed by Kedma Hamelberg, PhD. The projects are connected by their interest in human consequences, but each has its own sources, methods and limits.

AI Empowerment Observatory

Asks what retained AI reporting shows about human capability, creativity, autonomy, rights and control. It groups related source pages into developments and publishes weekly readings with source links and exclusions.

The Brief

Makes the Observatory’s news and separate research items easier to read. It links to original reporting or papers, labels limited evidence, and keeps a distinction between sourced claims and observed outcomes.

European Security Monitor

Examines European security narratives in sampled public videos and official communications. It displays source coverage, classifications, audience responses and limits to interpretation.

Creator and responsibility

Kedma Hamelberg, PhD, is an applied AI researcher, builder and educator. She created and developed the Observatory, The Brief and the European Security Monitor. Each project links to its own methodology and original materials so readers can trace and challenge a public claim.

AI-assisted collection, translation, drafting and classification can introduce errors. The sites describe where human review applies and where results remain provisional. Source availability varies by publisher, country and format. These limitations are part of the interpretation, not a footnote to remove.

Editorial approach and AI assistance

The root site publishes explanatory notes and reading tools alongside links to the three projects. The notes added on 14 November 2026 use AI-assisted drafting and source checking. They do not claim to be new empirical studies, and AI assistance should not be read as a guarantee of accuracy. Examples created to teach a method are labelled hypothetical and are kept separate from reported findings.

Factual descriptions link to the primary material where available. Interpretation is identified as interpretation. We do not label a page as personally reviewed by the publisher unless that review has actually occurred. The live projects publish their own collection, review and correction rules in their methodology pages.

Corrections and version dates

Send the page URL, the disputed statement and a supporting source to research@hamelberg-ai.com. A correction should preserve the distinction between a wrong source fact, a disputed interpretation and a changing underlying source. Substantive updates to explanatory notes should identify the affected claim and update the page date. A published date records publication, not an assurance that every linked source remains unchanged.

Editorial independence and contact

Advertising and support may help fund the public work. They do not buy a favourable summary, ranking or classification. Questions about a source, correction or partnership can be sent to research@hamelberg-ai.com. Include the page URL and the claim in question so it can be checked against the underlying material.
